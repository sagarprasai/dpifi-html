# Diverse Patterns Institute for Inclusion — Website Brief

Create a simple, welcoming website for **Diverse Patterns Institute for Inclusion**, a newly established nonprofit organization based in Kathmandu, Nepal.

The Institute aims to contribute to a more inclusive society by promoting disability inclusion, accessibility, learning, research, and knowledge sharing. As a new organization, the website should communicate its mission and intended work honestly, without suggesting that it already has a long history or extensive achievements.

## About the Organization

Diverse Patterns Institute for Inclusion works to promote the participation and inclusion of persons with disabilities in society. It aims to work with communities, organizations, public institutions, researchers, and disability-led organizations to identify barriers and explore practical ways to address them.

The Institute is inspired by the belief that everyone should have equal opportunities to learn, participate, access information, and contribute to their communities.

## What We Do

The Institute plans to focus on four main areas:

- **Inclusion and Accessibility:** Promoting accessible environments, information, communication, digital platforms, and opportunities for participation.
- **Research and Learning:** Conducting research, documenting experiences, and generating knowledge about disability inclusion and accessibility in Nepal.
- **Knowledge and Resources:** Developing and sharing guidelines, learning materials, practical tools, and other resources that support inclusion.
- **Collaboration and Awareness:** Working with communities, disability-led organizations, educational institutions, public bodies, and development organizations to promote understanding and inclusive practices.

Present these as the Institute’s areas of work, not as a commercial list of services.

## Who We Work With

The Institute seeks to collaborate with:

- Organizations of persons with disabilities and disability rights groups.
- Local communities, families, and individuals with disabilities.
- Schools, universities, and research institutions.
- Government bodies and public institutions.
- National and international development organizations.
- Researchers, educators, and professionals interested in inclusion and accessibility.

The website should make it clear that the Institute welcomes partnerships, shared learning, research collaboration, and opportunities to support disability inclusion.

## Our Approach

The Institute values participation, accessibility, collaboration, and learning from lived experiences. Persons with disabilities should be recognized as contributors and decision-makers, not simply as beneficiaries.

Its work will focus on understanding barriers, sharing knowledge, and supporting practical changes that make communities, institutions, and information more inclusive.

## Get Involved

Encourage visitors to connect with the Institute, explore opportunities for collaboration, share knowledge, and support its work.

Use simple calls to action such as:
- Learn About Our Work
- Explore Our Focus
- Work With Us
- Contact Us
- Support Our Work

## Website Content

Include basic information about the organization, its focus areas, opportunities for collaboration, contact details, and relevant organizational information.

The homepage may also highlight an actual recent activity when appropriate. Since the Institute is new, do not invent projects, publications, impact figures, testimonials, funding partners, or achievements. Emphasize its purpose, future direction, and willingness to collaborate.

## Visual Direction

Use the supplied Diverse Patterns Institute for Inclusion logo and its blue, magenta, and purple branding. The website should feel modern, approachable, and appropriate for a small nonprofit in Nepal.

Use inclusive illustrations or genuine photographs featuring people with disabilities in everyday community, educational, and collaborative settings. Keep the language simple, clear, and modest. Avoid corporate consulting language, exaggerated claims, and an overly elaborate institutional appearance.

The final website should help visitors quickly understand three things: **why the Institute exists, what it plans to work on, and how they can collaborate with or support it.**
